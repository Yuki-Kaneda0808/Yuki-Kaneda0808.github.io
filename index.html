YUKI KANEDA · REPRODUCTIVE & DEVELOPMENTAL BIOLOGY

From germline
to embryo.

Exploring how biological information is established, transmitted, and rewritten across generations.

My research began with dissecting gene function in mammalian spermatogenesis using mouse genome editing. I am now extending this causal approach across fertilization into early embryonic development and epigenetic reprogramming.

01 / TRAJECTORY

One question,
across generations.

My research follows biological information across the reproductive cycle — from genes controlling germ-cell differentiation to the molecular reprogramming that begins immediately after fertilization.

PAST 01

Germline biology

Understanding the molecular machinery underlying spermiogenesis, sperm function, and male fertility.

Spermiogenesis · Male fertility · Gene function
TOOLKIT 02

Mouse genome editing

Building genetically modified mouse models to perturb candidate genes and establish causal relationships between genotype and reproductive phenotype.

CRISPR · Mouse genetics · Functional analysis
NOW 03

Early embryo

Investigating how parental molecular information is reorganized after fertilization as a new developmental program emerges.

Fertilization · Chromatin · Epigenetic reprogramming
FORWARD 04

Epigenome engineering

Moving from observing developmental transitions toward perturbing epigenetic states and testing their causal roles in mammalian development.

Causality · Engineering · Development

02 / APPROACH

Perturb.
Observe.
Understand.

The biological system changes.
The experimental logic remains.

Genome editing provides a way to move beyond correlation: alter biological information, observe the resulting phenotype, and uncover mechanism.

I aim to extend this logic from reproductive genetics into the earliest stages of mammalian development.

CURRENT DIRECTION

What happens to biological information after fertilization?

The transition from two highly specialized gametes to a totipotent embryo requires profound reorganization of chromatin, DNA methylation, and transcriptional regulation. Understanding this transition is the next chapter of my research.
