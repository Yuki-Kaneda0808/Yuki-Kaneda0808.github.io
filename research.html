From genetic perturbation to developmental reprogramming.

I study biological information by perturbing it. My research is moving from gene function in the mammalian germline toward the molecular reprogramming that initiates embryonic development.

CENTRAL IDEA

Perturb the information.
Reveal the mechanism.

Different biological systems, one experimental logic.

Genetic perturbation makes it possible to move from association to causality. In the germline, I have used genetically modified mice to ask how individual genes control spermiogenesis, sperm architecture and fertility.

I am interested in extending this causal framework across fertilization — from perturbing DNA sequence to perturbing the epigenetic states that shape early development.

01 DISCOVER GERMLINE

Germline biology.

How does a round spermatid become a functional sperm cell?

My previous work focused on molecular mechanisms of spermiogenesis and male fertility. By disrupting testis-enriched genes in mice, I investigated how specialized proteins shape sperm morphology and function.

02 PERTURB MOUSE GENETICS

Mouse genome editing.

Build the perturbation. Read the phenotype.

Genetically modified mice provide a direct route from candidate gene to organismal phenotype. This approach has been central to my work on reproductive biology: disrupt a gene, characterize the resulting cellular phenotype, and identify the underlying mechanism.

03 / TRANSITION

Two genomes.
One developmental program.

Fertilization is not simply the meeting of two genomes. It initiates a radical transition in biological state.

Highly specialized gametes must give rise to an embryo capable of generating an entire organism. Parental genomes are reorganized, chromatin is remodeled, and developmental regulation is established anew.

04 REPROGRAM EARLY DEVELOPMENT

Early embryo epigenetics.

How is inherited molecular information rewritten after fertilization?

I am interested in the regulatory transitions that accompany the beginning of mammalian development, particularly the reorganization of chromatin and epigenetic information after fertilization.
